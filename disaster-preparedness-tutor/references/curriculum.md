# Curriculum

Reference for the disaster-preparedness-tutor skill. Teaching method, levels,
and evaluation rules live in `SKILL.md`; this file holds what is taught.

## Domains

(H) = hands-on subskill, has a strongly recommended field assignment.

### Layer 1 - Foundation

**1. Mindset and decision-making**
- Stress response and how to control it (breathing, tactical pause)
- Failure modes: normalcy bias, freeze, denial, bystander effect
- Situational awareness: baselines and anomalies
- Prioritizing under pressure: what kills first
- Stay-or-go decisions and pre-set triggers
- Mental rehearsal and if-then planning

**2. Risk assessment and planning**
- Personal hazard profile: likelihood x consequence
- Household plan: roles, rally points, out-of-area contact (H)
- Plans for work, school, and travel
- Planning for dependents, medical needs, pets
- Documents and digital backups (H)
- Insurance gaps and financial buffer, cash on hand

### Layer 2 - Core capabilities

**3. Medical**
- Scene safety, primary assessment, calling 911 well
- Severe bleeding: pressure, wound packing, tourniquet (H)
- CPR and AED: adult, child, infant (H)
- Choking: adult, child, infant (H)
- Shock, chest injury, recovery position (H)
- Burns, fractures, sprains, head and spine injury
- Heat and cold illness
- Medical emergencies: heart attack, stroke, anaphylaxis, overdose, seizure,
  diabetic emergency
- Delayed-help care: wound care, infection signs, dehydration, medication supply
- Psychological first aid
- Kits: personal trauma kit, home medical kit (H)

**4. Sheltering in place**
- Water: storage, sourcing, treatment (H)
- Food: storage, rotation, cooking without power
- Power and light: batteries, generators, carbon monoxide rules
- Heating and cooling without the grid
- Sanitation, hygiene, waste
- Utility shutoffs: gas, water, electric (H)
- Home hardening and sealing a room
- Smoke and CO alarms, extinguishers (H)
- Home supplies by tier

**5. Evacuation and mobility**
- Triggers and timing: leaving early
- Go-bag: contents and why (H)
- Vehicle readiness and vehicle kit (H)
- Routes, alternates, destinations
- Navigation without a phone: map and compass (H)
- Get-home plan from work or school
- Moving with kids, elderly, pets, limited mobility
- Public shelters: what to expect

**6. Field survival**
- Priorities: exposure first, food last
- Shelter, clothing, insulation (H)
- Fire in bad conditions (H)
- Finding and treating water (H)
- Signaling and getting found
- Staying found: navigation and lost-person behavior
- Knife, cordage, and tool basics (H)
- Improvising in an urban setting

**7. Communications and information**
- Alert systems: wireless alerts, NOAA Weather Radio, local sign-ups (H)
- Family communications plan: text first, check-in schedule, contact card
- When networks fail: FRS, GMRS, amateur radio, licensing (H)
- Satellite messengers
- Offline references and device power
- Information triage: verifying, spotting rumor

**8. Security and safety**
- Awareness habits in public
- Pre-incident indicators
- Avoidance and de-escalation
- Home security layers (H)
- Post-disaster crime, scams, contractor fraud
- Civil unrest: avoiding, exiting, sheltering
- Self-defense law concepts (state-specific)
- Digital and identity security
- Optional firearms track: only if chosen at intake. Teach safety rules, safe
  storage, legal concepts, and decision-making. Send handling and marksmanship
  to certified in-person instruction.

### Layer 3 - Scenario playbooks

Each hazard: before, during, after.

**9. Common emergencies**
- House fire: escape plan, two ways out, fight-or-flee (H)
- Carbon monoxide and gas leaks
- Extended power outage
- Winter storm and extreme cold
- Extreme heat
- Vehicle emergencies: crash, stranded, fire, submersion
- Water emergencies: burst pipe, boil notice
- Severe thunderstorm and lightning

**10. Active shooter and violent attack**
- Recognizing gunfire and the first seconds
- Run, Hide, Fight decision-making (also taught as Avoid, Deny, Defend)
- Exits, cover vs. concealment
- Lockdown and barricading (H)
- Venue differences: office, school, store, crowd, worship, vehicle
- Fighting as a last resort: commit fully
- When police arrive: hands, movement, information
- Bleeding control in the aftermath (ties to Domain 3)
- Other attacks: vehicle ramming, knife, explosive, secondary threats
- Reunification and aftermath
- Children: school drills, age-appropriate conversations

**11. Regional disasters**
- Earthquake
- Hurricane and storm surge
- Tornado
- Flood and flash flood
- Wildfire and smoke
- Tsunami, landslide, volcano where relevant

Only hazards in the user's hazard profile are full modules and count toward
the domain level. The rest get one combined overview module (for travel) that
does not count.

**12. Rare, high-consequence**
- Plane crash and aircraft emergencies (H):
  - Before: safety briefing and card, counting rows to two exits, clothing
    and shoes, seatbelt habits, takeoff and landing as the alert windows
  - During: brace position, oxygen masks (own first), smoke and fire,
    decompression, turbulence
  - Evacuation: leave everything, move fast, exits and slides, helping
    others without blocking
  - Water landing: life vest (inflate outside the cabin), rafts, cold water
  - After: getting clear of the aircraft, treating injuries, staying with the
    wreckage, signaling, survival until rescue (ties to Domains 3 and 6)
  - Flying with children
- Pandemic and infectious outbreak
- Hazardous materials release: shelter vs. evacuate
- Radiological and nuclear
- Long-term grid failure and cyberattack
- Supply chain and economic disruption

### Layer 4 - Sustainment

**13. Community and leadership**
- Leading a household under stress
- Children by age
- Elderly, disabled, medically dependent
- Pets and livestock
- Neighbors and mutual aid (H)
- Triage basics and directing bystanders
- CERT and volunteer training
- Teaching others

**14. Practice, maintenance, recovery**
- Drill calendar (H)
- Supply rotation and gear checks (H)
- Fitness baseline for emergencies
- Skill refresh and recertification
- After-action review
- Recovery: safe re-entry, documentation, insurance claims, federal aid, scams
- Mental recovery


## Default path

First five sessions, then: 1 -> 2 -> 3 (CPR, choking next) -> 9 -> 10 -> 4 -> 5
-> 7 -> rest of 3 -> 8 -> 11 -> 6 -> 13 -> 12 -> 14. Reorder for the hazard
profile. If the user has a flight coming up, offer the aircraft module first.
Gear is taught inside each domain: every tool gets why, how, and failure
modes.

## Readiness checklist items

Add each item when its module is taught (rules in `SKILL.md`).

Tier 1 (72 hours):
- Household plan written and briefed
- Contact card for every household member
- Local alerts set up
- Smoke and CO alarms tested
- Fire escape plan walked
- 72 hours of water
- 72 hours of food
- Light and phone power without the grid
- First-aid kit plus bleeding-control kit
- Go-bag per person
- Vehicle kit
- Copies of key documents
- Some cash on hand

Tier 2 (2 weeks):
- 2 weeks of water, plus a way to treat more
- 2 weeks of food and a way to cook without power
- Sanitation setup
- Medication buffer (through their doctor or pharmacist)
- Heating or cooling plan without the grid
- Weather radio
- Evacuation routes and destinations mapped
- Supplies for pets and dependents
- Utility shutoffs located and tools in place

Tier 3 (30+ days):
- 30+ day food and water plan
- Second, independent water treatment method
- Communication beyond the phone network
- Mutual-aid contact with at least one neighbor
- Insurance reviewed against the hazard profile
- Kits at work and school
- Yearly maintenance calendar
